Which file do I need?

The raw DNA data file (a .zip) from your DNA testing service. Not the PDF health report, not a screenshot. You don't need a new test.

Getting the file from 23andMe
  1. Sign in to 23andMe and open Settings → 23andMe Data → Download raw data (or use the button below).
  2. Confirm your date of birth, tick the box, and submit the request.
  3. 23andMe emails you when the download is ready. People often wait about a week; it can take up to 30 days.
  4. Download the genome_….zip file and upload it below.
Getting the file from Ancestry
  1. Sign in to Ancestry and open DNA → Settings → Download DNA data.
  2. Confirm the request, then open the link Ancestry emails you.
  3. Download the zip file and upload it below.

How your DNA shapes your plan

GeneMeal reads a short list of specific DNA markers from the file you upload, nothing more, and uses them to build a gene-based diet plan that fits you. Already took a DNA test for ancestry? The raw DNA file from ancestry or DNA testing services is all you need for an ancestry meal plan. GeneMeal does not analyze ancestry; it only reads nutrition markers.

  1. Upload. Add the raw DNA file from your testing service.
  2. We read your markers. We look only at the markers linked to how people handle certain foods.
  3. Your plan. You get daily food-group targets and menus you can build with tap-to-choose lists.

What your markers change

  • Daily targets: how much from each food group (grains, vegetables, fruits, dairy, protein, oils) you aim for each day.
  • Shown or hidden foods: foods that don't suit your markers are left out, so every choice on your list works for you.
  • Starred picks: foods that are an especially good match get a ★.
  • Portion sizes: each food comes in a few portion sizes, and ones that would push you past a target are greyed out.

A few examples

  • Lactose: if your markers suggest milk may not sit well, regular dairy is swapped for lactose-free milk, yogurt and fortified soy.
  • Saturated fat: if your markers point to fat sensitivity, richer portions are hidden and lean, unsaturated picks are starred.
  • Gluten / celiac risk: if you carry the gene type most linked to celiac disease, wheat, barley and rye are hidden and gluten-free grains stay.
  • Omega-3: if your body may make less omega-3 on its own, fish, walnuts, chia and flax get a star.
  • Appetite and fullness: if your markers link to a bigger appetite, filling high-fiber and high-protein foods are starred.

GeneMeal gives general nutrition guidance. It is not medical advice or a diagnosis. Talk to your doctor before making big changes to your diet.

Questions

What does GeneMeal read from my file?

Only a short list of nutrition-related markers (for example lactose, omega-3, saturated fat, gluten-related gene types and appetite). It does not analyze ancestry, disease risk, or anything else in the file. The full genotype table is not kept.

Is this medical advice?

No. GeneMeal gives general, educational nutrition guidance. It does not diagnose, treat, or prevent any condition, and genotype effects on diet are usually small. Talk with a doctor or registered dietitian before making big changes.

What happens to my DNA file?

The uploaded zip is encrypted on our server while you review your markers. When you confirm, the raw file is deleted and only the short marker notes are kept, also encrypted. You can delete everything at any time below or from the Privacy Policy page.

How do I cancel?

Online, anytime, from the Cancel subscription page: card subscriptions through Stripe's customer portal, PayPal subscriptions under Automatic payments in PayPal. Cancellation takes effect at the end of your current billing period. Deleting your data does not cancel billing on its own.

Why isn't GeneMeal available in my state?

Some states have genetic-privacy laws with requirements GeneMeal doesn't meet yet, so uploads, results and checkout are turned off for visitors from those {{ blocked_state_count }} states: {{ blocked_state_names | join(", ") }}. Reading pages like this one still works everywhere.

Do I need a new DNA test?

No. If you've already tested with 23andMe or Ancestry, the raw data file from that test is all you need.
