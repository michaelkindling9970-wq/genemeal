How your DNA shapes your plan

GeneMeal reads a short list of specific DNA markers from the file you upload, nothing more, and uses them to build a gene-based diet plan that fits you. Already took a DNA test for ancestry? The raw DNA file from ancestry or DNA testing services is all you need for an ancestry meal plan. GeneMeal does not analyze ancestry; it only reads nutrition markers.

  1. Upload. Add the raw DNA file from your testing service.
  2. We read your markers. We look only at the markers linked to how people handle certain foods.
  3. Your plan. You get daily food-group targets and menus you can build with tap-to-choose lists.

What your markers change

  • Daily targets: how much from each food group (grains, vegetables, fruits, dairy, protein, oils) you aim for each day.
  • Shown or hidden foods: foods that don't suit your markers are left out, so every choice on your list works for you.
  • Starred picks: foods that are an especially good match get a ★.
  • Portion sizes: each food comes in a few portion sizes, and ones that would push you past a target are greyed out.

A few examples

  • Lactose: if your markers suggest milk may not sit well, regular dairy is swapped for lactose-free milk, yogurt and fortified soy.
  • Saturated fat: if your markers point to fat sensitivity, richer portions are hidden and lean, unsaturated picks are starred.
  • Gluten / celiac risk: if you carry the gene type most linked to celiac disease, wheat, barley and rye are hidden and gluten-free grains stay.
  • Omega-3: if your body may make less omega-3 on its own, fish, walnuts, chia and flax get a star.
  • Appetite and fullness: if your markers link to a bigger appetite, filling high-fiber and high-protein foods are starred.

GeneMeal gives general nutrition guidance. It is not medical advice or a diagnosis. Talk to your doctor before making big changes to your diet.
